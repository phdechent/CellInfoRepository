# Capacity Degradation

**Summary:** Capacity degradation (fade of the dischargeable capacity, usually expressed as SOH or capacity retention) is tracked in the 2025 open-access corpus on commercial NCA, NMC and LFP cylindrical cells under randomized, low-temperature, self-heating, second-life, X-ray-exposed and relaxation-dependent test regimes. The papers differ strongly in ambient control, mechanical fixturing and how much of the measured loss is truly irreversible.

## Synthesized Knowledge

**Protocols and boundary conditions.** Capacity is typically measured in periodic capacity checks or reference performance tests (RPTs) interleaved with aging cycles, but the check-up design varies widely. The randomized-current dataset on JGNE JGPFR26650 (LFP), Samsung ICR18650-26J (NCA) and Samsung INR21700-40T (NMC) cells computes SOH from periodic capacity tests normalised to nominal capacity, with HPPC every 20 cycles, over more than 600 cycles at an unspecified room temperature, with each cell in a clamp (magnitude not reported) and one T-type thermocouple at the surface centre sampled at 1 Hz (Source: [[dataset-of-lithium-ion-cell-degradation-under-randomized-current-profi]]). The second-life study of Samsung INR18650-35E cells uses a 20 °C setpoint in a temperature-controlled, insulated steel container, noting that "some variation was observed", with custom 18650 holders (welded joints, 4-probe contacts), one thermocouple per cell, and capacity/DCIR/EIS checks every 50 or 100 cycles (Source: [[impact-of-first-life-usage-on-second-life-performance-of-lithium-ion-b]]). The X-ray exposure study on EVE INR18650-33V cells ran RPTs at 33 °C and accelerated cycling at 45 °C in convection chambers, with five cells per group (Source: [[lab-scale-x-ray-exposure-has-no-measurable-impact-on-lithium-ion-batte]]). The Samsung INR21700-50S machine-learning study cycled at 1C for 300 cycles at 25 °C in a climatic chamber, using surface temperature as a model input (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]). The Samsung INR21700-50G relaxation study used 25 °C climate chambers "without additional active temperature control", Arbin single-cell holders with no reported pressure, and NTC thermistors on the cell surface; the authors could not rule out small temperature differences between cells caused by their position in the chamber (Source: [[the-overlooked-role-of-battery-cell-relaxation-how-reversible-effects]]). None of these papers reports a quantified mechanical pressure.

**Temperature-dependent aging.** For Sony US18650VTC6 cells cycled at 3C/3C, lower temperature sped up capacity loss: after 150 cycles 83.3 % was retained at 25 °C versus 74.2 % at −20 °C (chamber accuracy ±2 °C). Resistance growth did not follow one trend: polarization resistance grew 28.9 % at −20 °C versus 13.5 % at 25 °C, whereas ohmic resistance grew 15.4 % at 25 °C, 19.7 % at 0 °C and only 12.4 % at −20 °C, and total resistance growth peaked at −10 °C (20.8 %) (Source: [[research-on-aging-evolution-and-safety-characteristics-of-lithium-ion]]). The same paper gives two different sets of test temperatures: −20, −10, 0, 25 and 45 °C in Table 3, but −20, −10, 0, 10 and 25 °C in the capacity analysis (Source: [[research-on-aging-evolution-and-safety-characteristics-of-lithium-ion]]). For Panasonic-Sanyo NCR18650GA cells, reference cells discharged at about −9.6 °C also aged faster than those at about 25 °C, even at lower current (92.8–93.4 % vs about 94.8 % after 100 measurement cycles). Periodically AC-heated cells kept only 86.3–86.9 %, which the authors put down to mechanical stress from rapid temperature changes rather than to the alternating current itself. **Contradiction in direction:** in the continuous-heating tests at 25 °C ambient in the same study, cells with a *higher* average temperature showed *higher* capacity fade (about 94.1 % heated vs 94.8 % reference) (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-the-impact-of-high-frequency-alternating]]). So in this corpus, colder cycling speeds up fade below room temperature, while warmer operation speeds up fade above it. The papers do not reconcile the two regimes.

**Reversible vs. irreversible loss and test-design effects.** The INR21700-50G study shows that continuous cycling builds up lithiation and conducting-salt inhomogeneities. These cause partly reversible capacity loss that recovers during check-ups and 10 h rests. Shorter cycling interruptions gave up to four times fewer cycles before reaching 80 % capacity, larger DOD and higher C-rates made inhomogenization worse, lithium plating appeared shortly before the knee point, and static or dynamic recovery cycles roughly doubled cycle life. At 2C/2C, stronger self-heating distorted the capacity-difference metric (Source: [[the-overlooked-role-of-battery-cell-relaxation-how-reversible-effects]]). So capacity-fade values from accelerated tests depend on the protocol and are not purely a property of the cell. In second life, INR18650-35E cells lost about 2 % per 1,000 cycles under high-current second-life cycling and 0.6 % per 1,000 cycles in the HC-LC group, while the LC-LC group showed a capacity *increase*. First-life current level had no significant influence under identical second-life conditions (Source: [[impact-of-first-life-usage-on-second-life-performance-of-lithium-ion-b]]). X-ray doses of about 10 Gy and 300 Gy gave no statistically significant difference in capacity retention, rate capability or swelling over 300 cycles at 45 °C. The authors caution that this may not hold for other formats, chemistries or beyond 300 cycles (Source: [[lab-scale-x-ray-exposure-has-no-measurable-impact-on-lithium-ion-batte]]). Humidity exposure during teardown of A123 ANR26650M1-B cells produced coin-cell capacity fade at C/10, with LFP fading more slowly than NCM-LMO from Sony US18650V3 cells (Source: [[raman-spectroscopy-of-practical-lib-cathodes-a-study-of-humidity-induc]]).

**Modelling capacity fade.** Only one referencing paper models capacity fade directly. It uses random forest and SVR regressors trained on current, voltage, surface temperature and voltage rate of change from a single INR21700-50S cell at 1C and 25 °C. Random forest reached R² 0.94 and RMSE 0.0570, against R² 0.909 and RMSE 0.0638 for SVR. The model has no thermal component and no spatial resolution, and it is reported to be more accurate above 50 % SOH (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]). The NCR18650GA study uses an equivalent circuit model with constant resistances only to estimate heater currents, not to predict aging (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-the-impact-of-high-frequency-alternating]]).

## Literature Mentions

- [[dataset-of-lithium-ion-cell-degradation-under-randomized-current-profi]]: Open dataset of >600 randomized-current cycles with HPPC every 20 cycles on JGNE JGPFR26650, Samsung ICR18650-26J and INR21700-40T cells at unspecified room temperature, clamped.
- [[experimental-investigation-of-the-impact-of-high-frequency-alternating]]: Periodic AC self-heating from about −9 °C of Panasonic-Sanyo NCR18650GA cells gave 86.3–86.9 % retention vs 92.8–93.4 % for reference cells, attributed to thermal-cycling mechanical stress.
- [[impact-of-first-life-usage-on-second-life-performance-of-lithium-ion-b]]: Retired Samsung INR18650-35E cells showed second-life fade of about 2 %/1,000 cycles (HC) to a capacity increase (LC-LC), independent of first-life current, at a 20 °C setpoint.
- [[lab-scale-x-ray-exposure-has-no-measurable-impact-on-lithium-ion-batte]]: 2 min and 60 min CT exposure had no measurable effect on capacity fade of EVE INR18650-33V cells over 300 cycles at 45 °C.
- [[raman-spectroscopy-of-practical-lib-cathodes-a-study-of-humidity-induc]]: Humidity-exposed LFP cathodes from A123 ANR26650M1-B cells showed coin-cell capacity fade at C/10, slower than NCM-LMO.
- [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]: Random forest predicted SOH of a Samsung INR21700-50S cell (300 cycles, 1C, 25 °C) with R² 0.94, outperforming SVR.
- [[research-on-aging-evolution-and-safety-characteristics-of-lithium-ion]]: Sony US18650VTC6 cells retained 74.2 % at −20 °C vs 83.3 % at 25 °C after 150 3C/3C cycles, with non-monotonic resistance growth vs temperature.
- [[the-overlooked-role-of-battery-cell-relaxation-how-reversible-effects]]: Samsung INR21700-50G cells show reversible capacity loss from inhomogenization; test interruptions and recovery cycles change cycle life by up to a factor of four.
