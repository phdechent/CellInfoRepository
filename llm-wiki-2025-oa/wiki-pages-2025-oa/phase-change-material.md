# Phase Change Material

**Summary:** In the 2025 open-access corpus, phase change materials (PCMs; paraffins/Rubitherm grades with melting ranges of roughly 38–48 °C) are studied as passive or hybrid (PCM–air, PCM–liquid) cooling for small packs of commercial 18650 cells, evaluated by maximum surface temperature (Tmax), cell-to-cell temperature difference and PCM melting. All four studies use own pack-level constant-current discharge experiments with surface thermocouples; only one adds a validated 3D model.

## Synthesized Knowledge

**PCM concepts and headline results.** On a 3S3P pack of Sony-Murata US18650VTC5 cells, Rubitherm RT47 (melting range 41–48 °C) with extended copper fins in an air duct lowered Tmax to 55 °C at 3C and 3 m/s and reduced the maximum temperature difference by about 39–62% relative to forced-air cooling, keeping it within 5 °C (Source: [[a-novel-hybrid-cooling-system-for-a-lithium-ion-battery-pack-based-on]]). A nine-cell series pack of Samsung SDI INR18650-25R cells filled with 40–42 °C paraffin and cooled by aluminium air pipes stayed below 42 °C at 3C across 22–42 °C ambient, while the air-only baseline rose from 38 °C to 55 °C; the PCM reduced Tmax by 2.6–13.3 °C at 3C but only 1.6–2.5 °C at 1C (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]). For a 3s2p Panasonic-Sanyo NCR18650GA pack in RT42/expanded-graphite (PCM/EG, about 90% PCM) blocks, electrolytic Ni-P and Ni-P-Cu coatings raised composite thermal conductivity from 5.67 to 16.5 and 27.1 W/m·K; at 2.5C the uncoated pack peaked at approx. 61 °C with approx. 900 s discharge, whereas the Ni-P-Cu-coated pack stabilised at approx. 53 °C with approx. 1400 s discharge (Source: [[electrolytic-ni-p-and-ni-p-cu-coatings-on-pcm-loaded-expanded-graphite]]). In a 3p Samsung SDI INR18650-30Q pack at 2C, PCM gave the lowest maximum surface temperature (52.7 °C vs. 126.1 °C air-cooled, a 47% reduction) and 56 min of isothermal maintenance compared with silicone oil, thermally conductive adhesive and air, and adding an optimised indirect liquid channel (layout D) lowered surface temperature by a further 9 °C (17.8%) (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]).

**Boundary conditions and temperature sensing.** Ambient control differs across studies: a controlled room at 35 ± 1 °C (Source: [[a-novel-hybrid-cooling-system-for-a-lithium-ion-battery-pack-based-on]]), a 227 L thermal chamber stepping inlet air from 22 to 42 °C with ±0.5 °C accuracy and the pack initially at 22 ± 0.1 °C (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]), a conditioned chamber at 25 °C with stability not reported (Source: [[electrolytic-ni-p-and-ni-p-cu-coatings-on-pcm-loaded-expanded-graphite]]), and a chamber at 20 °C with ±0.3 °C stated error (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]). Mechanical constraint is either not discussed or limited to fixtures: the NCR18650GA cells sit in 18 mm cell spaces of the PCM/EG block with 5 mm 3D-printed top/bottom supports and no reported pressure (Source: [[electrolytic-ni-p-and-ni-p-cu-coatings-on-pcm-loaded-expanded-graphite]]). Cell temperature is measured exclusively by surface thermocouples: five K-type at 60 s sampling (Source: [[a-novel-hybrid-cooling-system-for-a-lithium-ion-battery-pack-based-on]]), nine T-type (±0.5 °C) at mid-height with unreported sampling (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]), six T-type on outer and inner surfaces of one cell (Source: [[electrolytic-ni-p-and-ni-p-cu-coatings-on-pcm-loaded-expanded-graphite]]), and three K-type per cell (top/middle/bottom) plus enclosure and PCM-centre sensors at 1 s sampling (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]). Temperature-dependent aging is not addressed by any of the four studies.

**PCM utilisation and ambient-temperature dependence.** The INR18650-25R study explicitly quantifies local PCM melting as ambient temperature approaches the melting point (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]), while the INR18650-30Q study reports that only about 37% of the PCM had melted after 30 min at 2C (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]). Reported limitations include added PCM weight, system complexity and limited PCM thermal conductivity (Source: [[a-novel-hybrid-cooling-system-for-a-lithium-ion-battery-pack-based-on]]), and the inability of the hybrid PCM–liquid system to keep temperatures within limits at ≥4C, with operation ≤2C advised (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]).

**Models.** The VTC5 study uses a lumped pack energy balance (Joule plus entropic heat generation, air convection, PCM storage) without calibration and compares its 3C temperature rise with literature at deviations of about 13–49% (Source: [[a-novel-hybrid-cooling-system-for-a-lithium-ion-battery-pack-based-on]]). The INR18650-30Q study uses a 3D STAR-CCM+ model with the NTGK electro-thermal battery model and an enthalpy-porosity PCM model (1 mm mesh), validated against its own 2C measurement (50.6 °C predicted vs. 51.2 °C measured, error under 1%), but assumes constant battery thermal conductivity and specific heat, and its channel, flow-rate and 1C–5C cases are purely numerical (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]). The two remaining studies contain no model (Source: [[electrolytic-ni-p-and-ni-p-cu-coatings-on-pcm-loaded-expanded-graphite]]; [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]).

**Contradictions and inconsistencies.** The air-cooled/no-medium baselines differ drastically: 126.1 °C for the INR18650-30Q 3p pack at 2C and 20 °C ambient (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]) versus 38–55 °C for the INR18650-25R pack at 3C with 2.1 m/s forced air at 22–42 °C (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]); the configurations (no medium vs. forced air) are not equivalent, so the values should not be compared directly. The INR18650-30Q paper describes its PCM as paraffin wax with a 40 °C melting point while its property table lists 1-tetradecanol, and its PCM-pack Tmax is reported as 52.7 °C in the media comparison but 51.2 °C in the validation measurement (Source: [[hybrid-pcm-liquid-cooling-system-with-optimized-channel-design-for-enh]]). The NCR18650GA study states discharge rates of 1.25C and 2.5C but currents of 8.1 A and 10.2 A for a 6700 mAh pack, which are not mutually consistent as reported, and at 1.25C the Ni-P coating gave a higher peak (approx. 45 °C) than the uncoated PCM/EG (approx. 43 °C) (Source: [[electrolytic-ni-p-and-ni-p-cu-coatings-on-pcm-loaded-expanded-graphite]]). PCM benefit is strongly C-rate dependent: small at 1C (1.6–2.5 °C) (Source: [[experimental-investigation-of-phase-change-material-based-battery-pack]]).
