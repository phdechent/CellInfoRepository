# Machine Learning

**Summary:** Across ten 2025 open-access studies with newly measured data on commercial cells, machine learning is used as a data-driven estimator for state of health, state of charge, remaining capacity and even cell chemistry, typically trained on a small number of cells tested at one or a few fixed chamber temperatures. None of the ML models contains a thermal component or spatial resolution; temperature, where used at all, is only an input feature.

## Synthesized Knowledge

**Model families and targets.** Recurrent networks dominate health estimation: a multi-input metabolic LSTM (MM-LSTM) for online SoH of Samsung ICR18650-26F cells (Source: [[a-novel-online-state-of-health-estimation-method-for-lithium-ion-batte]]), a multi-input metabolic GRU (MM-GRU) for retired Samsung ICR18650-26F and Panasonic NCR18650BD cells and six-cell packs (Source: [[the-state-of-health-estimation-of-retired-lithium-ion-batteries-using]]), an RNN estimating static capacity of Samsung INR21700-40T cells from 6-min 1C partial discharges (Source: [[machine-learning-based-methodology-for-fast-assessment-of-battery-heal]]), and an ANN fed by automatically extracted incremental-capacity features (DoG-TVA) for Lishen LP27148134 and CATL CB2W0 LFP cells (Source: [[smart-health-evaluation-for-lithium-ion-battery-with-super-short-segme]]). A GRU classifier identifies chemistry (NCA-GS Sony-Murata US18650VTC6 vs. NCA-G vs. NMC-G) solely from normalized surface-temperature profiles, with 97.5–100% accuracy (Source: [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]). Tree ensembles dominate the remaining work: random forest vs. SVR for SoH of a Samsung INR-21700-50S cell (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]), linear regression and random forest on engineered features of 256 Samsung INR21700-50E cells (Source: [[data-on-battery-health-and-performance-analysing-samsung-inr21700-50e]]), a two-layer random forest for capacity identification and SOC of second-life Samsung ICR18650-22P cells (Source: [[self-soc-estimation-for-second-life-lithium-ion-batteries]]), random forest vs. SVR, linear regression, Coulomb counting and an EKF for SOC of an A123 Systems 26AH pouch cell (Source: [[soc-estimation-for-a-lithium-ion-pouch-cell-using-machine-learning-und]]), and an XGBoost–random forest stack with a dual-state Kalman filter for a 20S1P Samsung INR18650-25R e-bike pack (Source: [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]). Where random forest was compared directly, it was reported as the best performer (RMSE 0.0229 for SOC of the A123 26AH (Source: [[soc-estimation-for-a-lithium-ion-pouch-cell-using-machine-learning-und]]); R² 0.94 vs. 0.909 for SVR on the INR-21700-50S (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]])).

**Experimental boundary conditions of the training data.** Ambient temperature control is heterogeneous. Chamber-controlled single temperatures are reported at 25 °C for the INR21700-50E (Source: [[data-on-battery-health-and-performance-analysing-samsung-inr21700-50e]]), the INR-21700-50S (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]) and the US18650VTC6 (insulated with 1.5 cm styrofoam) (Source: [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]), at 24 °C for the retired ICR18650-26F/NCR18650BD cells (Source: [[the-state-of-health-estimation-of-retired-lithium-ion-batteries-using]]), and at 25 °C with a stated tolerance of ±0.2 °C (plus a 10 h 45 °C stress) for the Lishen/CATL cells (Source: [[smart-health-evaluation-for-lithium-ion-battery-with-super-short-segme]]). Only the MM-LSTM study used several controlled temperatures (characteristic tests at 10, 25, 35 and 40 °C; aging at 35 and 45 °C) (Source: [[a-novel-online-state-of-health-estimation-method-for-lithium-ion-batte]]). The INR21700-40T data were taken only at unspecified room temperature (Source: [[machine-learning-based-methodology-for-fast-assessment-of-battery-heal]]); ambient temperature is not stated for the ICR18650-22P (Source: [[self-soc-estimation-for-second-life-lithium-ion-batteries]]) or the A123 26AH (Source: [[soc-estimation-for-a-lithium-ion-pouch-cell-using-machine-learning-und]]), and the numeric temperatures of the INR18650-25R pack tests are not recoverable (Source: [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]). Cell temperature sensing is sparse: a single PT100 mid-way on the VTC6 surface (Source: [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]), an unspecified surface sensor on the INR-21700-50S (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]), an unspecified "internal" cell temperature channel at 100 Hz/1 kHz for the INR21700-50E (Source: [[data-on-battery-health-and-performance-analysing-samsung-inr21700-50e]]), and BMS sensors at 1 Hz for the INR18650-25R pack (Source: [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]). No study in this group reports a mechanical boundary condition.

**Temperature effects and validation.** Temperature enters ML studies mostly indirectly. The MM-LSTM study uses the ohmic-resistance increment rather than absolute resistance because ohmic resistance of the ICR18650-26F is significantly influenced by ambient temperature (Source: [[a-novel-online-state-of-health-estimation-method-for-lithium-ion-batte]]); in the INR18650-25R study, HEAD-KF error rose only modestly at the lower temperature bound while LSTM error grew more strongly, and RC-EKF drift was attributed to temperature-dependent impedance (Source: [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]); for the VTC6, entropic heat produces the chemistry-specific surface-temperature signature used for classification (Source: [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]). Validation sets are small: training on a single cell or pack and testing on others (Source: [[the-state-of-health-estimation-of-retired-lithium-ion-batteries-using]]; Source: [[smart-health-evaluation-for-lithium-ion-battery-with-super-short-segme]]), three training/one validation/one test cell (Source: [[machine-learning-based-methodology-for-fast-assessment-of-battery-heal]]), or random splits of time-series samples from one cell (Source: [[soc-estimation-for-a-lithium-ion-pouch-cell-using-machine-learning-und]]; Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]). Reported limitations are that data were taken only at room temperature (Source: [[machine-learning-based-methodology-for-fast-assessment-of-battery-heal]]), that dynamic temperature and pack-level conditions were not investigated (Source: [[smart-health-evaluation-for-lithium-ion-battery-with-super-short-segme]]), that ambient-temperature effects were ignored due to insulation (Source: [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]), that harsh-temperature tests are still planned (Source: [[data-on-battery-health-and-performance-analysing-samsung-inr21700-50e]]), and that aged cells and other chemistries such as LFP remain unconfirmed (Source: [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]).

**Contradictions.** (1) Real-time feasibility: the A123 26AH study states that ML techniques need significant computing resources and training data, posing obstacles for real-time deployment (Source: [[soc-estimation-for-a-lithium-ion-pouch-cell-using-machine-learning-und]]), whereas the INR18650-25R stack runs on a Raspberry Pi 4 in about 6 ms per update (Source: [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]) and the ICR18650-22P random forests were run in real time on a Raspberry Pi, albeit with higher errors than offline (SOC RMSE 1.8% vs. 0.85%) (Source: [[self-soc-estimation-for-second-life-lithium-ion-batteries]]). (2) Accuracy range with degradation: the INR-21700-50S random forest is more accurate above 50% SoH (Source: [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]), while the VTC6 chemistry classifier is only validated down to about 75% SOH and may fail below it (Source: [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]); these state different, non-comparable validity limits for different tasks. (3) The same cell type, Samsung ICR18650-26F, is used by both metabolic recurrent models but under different conditions (fresh cells aged at 35/45 °C with dynamic profiles vs. retired cells at 24 °C under energy-storage profiles) and with different reported maximum errors (≤1.98% vs. below 3.8%) (Source: [[a-novel-online-state-of-health-estimation-method-for-lithium-ion-batte]]; Source: [[the-state-of-health-estimation-of-retired-lithium-ion-batteries-using]]); the errors are not directly comparable.

## Literature Mentions

- [[a-novel-online-state-of-health-estimation-method-for-lithium-ion-batte]]: MM-LSTM online SoH for Samsung ICR18650-26F (and Panasonic NCR18650B) aged at 35/45 °C; max error within 1.98% including cross-chemistry transfer.
- [[accurate-chemistry-identification-of-lithium-ion-batteries-based-on-te]]: GRU classifies Sony-Murata US18650VTC6 (NCA-GS) vs. NCA-G and NMC-G from a single PT100 surface temperature at 25 °C with 97.5–100% accuracy.
- [[data-on-battery-health-and-performance-analysing-samsung-inr21700-50e]]: Public dataset of 256 Samsung INR21700-50E cells at 25 °C; engineered features reduce linear-regression and random-forest errors.
- [[machine-learning-based-methodology-for-fast-assessment-of-battery-heal]]: RNN estimates static capacity of Samsung INR21700-40T from 6-min 1C partial discharges (RMSE 28.439 mAh), room temperature only.
- [[random-forest-based-machine-learning-model-design-for-21-700-5-ah-lith]]: Random forest outperforms SVR for SoH of a Samsung INR-21700-50S cycled 300 times at 1C and 25 °C (R² 0.94).
- [[self-soc-estimation-for-second-life-lithium-ion-batteries]]: Two-layer random forest identifies capacity and SOC of second-life Samsung ICR18650-22P cells, deployed in real time on a Raspberry Pi.
- [[smart-health-evaluation-for-lithium-ion-battery-with-super-short-segme]]: DoG-TVA IC features plus ANN estimate SOH of Lishen LP27148134 and CATL CB2W0 LFP cells from segments down to 10% DOD (RMSE 1.97%).
- [[soc-estimation-for-a-lithium-ion-pouch-cell-using-machine-learning-und]]: Random forest gives lowest SOC error (RMSE 0.0229) for the A123 Systems 26AH pouch cell vs. Coulomb counting, EKF, LR and SVR.
- [[the-state-of-health-estimation-of-retired-lithium-ion-batteries-using]]: MM-GRU estimates SoH of retired Samsung ICR18650-26F and Panasonic NCR18650BD cells and packs at 24 °C from four cycles of history.
- [[xgboost-random-forest-stacking-with-dual-state-kalman-filtering-for-re]]: XGBoost–random forest stack with dual-state Kalman filter estimates SOC of a Samsung INR18650-25R 20S1P e-bike pack in ~6 ms on a Raspberry Pi 4.
