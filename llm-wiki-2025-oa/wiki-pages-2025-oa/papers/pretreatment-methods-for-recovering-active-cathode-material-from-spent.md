# Pretreatment Methods for Recovering Active Cathode Material from Spent Lithium-Ion Batteries

## General Analysis

**Core Thesis:** The study compares thermal, NaOH-leaching, NMP and triethyl phosphate (TEP) pretreatments for recovering NMC532 cathode active material from disassembled Samsung SDI INR18650-20R cells, first on cathode sheets and then on entire cells, including a cycled cell. TEP pretreatment gave high recovery efficiency with preserved stoichiometry and particle morphology (about 8 µm mean diameter).

**Methodological Focus**
- Testing Mode: Cell discharge and teardown, TGA, ICP-OES, SEM/EDS with ImageJ particle analysis, XRD of recovered powders
- Operating Conditions: Pretreatment process conditions 25–120 °C solvent/leaching steps and 350–800 °C muffle treatments; cell operating temperature not reported
- Degradation Markers: Cycled cell: slightly smaller recovered particle diameter (7.93 µm) and lower (003)/(101) XRD intensity ratio (1.45)

## Keyword Context

- Direct Recycling: The work targets direct recycling, in which cathode active material is recovered without breaking down its crystal structure.
- Cathode Material Recovery: Recovery efficiency, element recovery, impurity levels and morphology of NMC532 recovered from the INR18650-20R cathode are compared across pretreatments; TEP reached 97% on cathode sheets.
- [[cell-teardown-analysis|Cell Teardown Analysis]]: Cells were discharged, opened and separated into cathode, anode and separator, whose compositions were characterized by TGA and ICP-OES.
